# Stitch Interactions from in_response_to edges, ordered by created_at

The twcs dataset records each tweet's reply target (`in_response_to_tweet_id`, single and reliable) alongside a `response_tweet_id` list that is incomplete and can include indirect mentions; we reconstruct SpotifyCares Interactions by walking `in_response_to_tweet_id` only and ordering turns by parsed `created_at` timestamps, because tweet ids are not monotonic with reply time (55% of reply edges invert id order) while timestamps never do.

Seeds are inbound tweets that engage the brand: they mention the handle anywhere in the text (whole-handle match, so `@SpotifyCaresHelp` does not match), were replied to by the brand, or reply to the brand. Mention-anywhere (rather than text-start only) was chosen after measuring both rules on the full dataset: restricting to text-start drops 209 genuine customer turns — often the true opening message of the issue — while the emitted Interaction set is identical either way, because any chain containing a brand turn always seeds via adjacency as well. From each seed we climb to the opening Customer Message — passing *through* intervening brand turns (a customer answering a brand question continues the same Interaction) and through same-author ancestors that also engage the brand (customer self-reply chains). The opening then grows downward into a dyad restricted to the customer author and the brand, so third-party interlopers start their own Interactions instead of polluting the customer's. An opening that lands inside another opening's dyad is absorbed as a continuation rather than emitted, because the climb (which requires brand engagement) and dyad growth (which requires only authorship) can otherwise disagree on a mid-chain detail tweet and double-claim its turns. An Interaction requires at least one brand turn: without a brand reply, no closure verdict (per ADR-0001) can ever be assigned, so such chains are reported as unanswered openings rather than emitted.

## Consequences

- Counts reported per stage (seeds → openings → Interactions → turns) keep the report honest about what was dropped and why.
- The JSONL output (`data/interactions.jsonl`) is the input contract for the English filter (ticket 4) and sampling (ticket 5); those stages must not re-parse twcs.csv.
- CONTEXT.md says a returning customer's next message always starts a new Interaction; for historical reconstruction that boundary is the reply chain — a reply-linked continuation (even days later) stays in one Interaction, while a fresh customer root starts a new one. Splitting in-chain continuations would double-count turns and leave fragments no closure verdict could attach to.
- The JSONL turns format is the stitching-stage record, not the final case format: specs-v0 §3's `{conversation_id, customer_message, brand_reply, intent, resolution}` is derived downstream, once closure labeling (tickets 6–8) and intent taxonomy (tickets 9+) supply `resolution` and `intent`. Every §3 field is derivable from the recorded turns.
